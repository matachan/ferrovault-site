Help & FAQ

Plain-English answers about FerroVault — what it is, how to set it up, and where your photos really live.

What is FerroVault?

Your own private cloud for your memories — running on a computer you own.

FerroVault is a self-hosted photo & video library. Instead of your photos living on a big company's servers, they live on your own computer, in a folder you control. Think of it as your own Google Photos or iCloud Photos — except the data never leaves your home unless you decide it should.

You run a small server app on a Mac (Windows & Linux are coming), your iPhone backs up to it over your home Wi‑Fi, and you browse everything from a web dashboard in any browser — or from the FerroVault iOS app.

How it works

Three simple pieces that work together on your home network.

The server

Your library's home

A small app you run on your always-on computer (such as a Mac). It stores and organizes your library. On a Mac, it lives quietly in your menu bar.

The dashboard

Where you browse

A web page you open from the menu bar to browse photos, albums, and manage your library. Other devices on your network can reach it too, using your computer's address.

The iOS app

Backup & browse

Backs up your iPhone's photos to your server, and lets you browse your whole library on the go while you're on your network.

What can it do today?

An honest list of what works right now.

  • Backs up iPhone photos & videos to your own server — chunked and resumable, tested with large 25 GB+ libraries — while the app is open and your phone is awake.
  • Full support for Apple formats: HEIC/HEIF, Live Photos, RAW, and video, plus JPEG and PNG.
  • Web dashboard to browse, search, organize albums, see per-device views, and check storage insights.
  • De-duplication so the same photo isn't stored twice — and a trustworthy "X of Y backed up" picture of your library.
  • Multi-device & multi-user: pair phones in seconds with QR sign-in, and share albums.
  • Menu-bar app on Mac with a Quick Glance panel: storage used and free, library size, server status, and a one-click jump to the dashboard.
  • Your data is yours: a backup and restore you can verify, and nothing uploaded to any third party.

A quick note on automatic backup: for now, syncing happens while the app is open — a set-and-watch flow with live progress. Fully automatic background backup is on the way; today the reliable path is leaving the app open while it syncs.

Installing on Mac Coming soon

The download isn't public yet — but here's exactly what to expect when it is.

1 Download & open FerroVault.dmg

Download FerroVault.dmg (coming soon) and double-click to open it.

2 Drag it into Applications

Drag FerroVault into your Applications folder, just like any Mac app.

3 Launch it

Open FerroVault. It appears in your menu bar (top-right of your screen) and starts your server automatically. Because the app is notarized by Apple, it opens normally — no scary "unidentified developer" warnings.

4 Open the dashboard & set up

Click the menu-bar icon, choose Open Dashboard, and follow the quick setup to pick where your library lives and create your account.

5 Turn on Start at Login (optional)

Flip on Start at Login so your server is always ready whenever your phone wants to back up.

Uninstalling on Mac

Uninstalling never deletes your photos — they're stored separately and stay put.

1 Quit FerroVault

Click the menu-bar icon, turn off Start at Login (if it's on), then choose Quit FerroVault.

2 Move the app to the Trash

Drag FerroVault from your Applications folder to the Trash.

3 Remove its settings (optional)

For a clean slate, you can delete the app's settings folder at ~/Library/Application Support/FerroVault. This is optional.

4 Your photos stay safe

Your library and photos are separate and are NOT removed. They remain in the library folder you chose. Only delete that folder yourself if you also want to erase your media.

Common questions

The everyday stuff — answered simply.

Q Where are my photos actually stored?

On your own computer, in the library folder you pick. They're never sent to FerroVault or any cloud company.

Q If I uninstall the app, do I lose my photos?

No. The app and your photo library are separate — uninstalling removes the app, not your media.

Q How do I open the dashboard from my phone or another computer?

Use your server's network address — your computer's IP, for example http://192.168.1.50:5256. The Mac menu bar has a Copy LAN URL button for exactly this. (localhost only works on the computer running the server.)

Q Does the server need to stay running?

Yes — keep the app running (turning on Start at Login makes this effortless). Your phone backs up whenever it can reach the server on your network.

Q Is it private and secure?

Your photos stay on your network unless you choose otherwise. The Mac app is Apple-notarized, and access requires your account.

Q What photo formats does it handle?

HEIC/HEIF, Live Photos, RAW, JPEG, PNG, and video.

Q Can my family use it too?

Yes — it supports multiple users and devices. Pair a new phone in seconds with a QR code.

Q Do my iPhone photos upload as weird HEIC files my PC can't open?

FerroVault keeps your originals and can show and serve them properly. An export-to-JPEG option is on the roadmap.

Q What happens if my disk gets full?

The menu-bar Quick Glance shows storage used and free, and warns you before you run out.
